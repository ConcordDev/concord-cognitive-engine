# Stories — the Court that eats steel and feeds birds

## Ecology

The Unburned Court is the old battlefield left unpaved, ringed by the gates Concord built and the Sovereign refused to close. Concordia poured herself into this ground. In Year 38 a coalition held the plaza for four hours, and she answered with a tide of root and bloom that carried them out without a death and without a punishment. The cobble still holds that seed in its cracks. That is the start of the food web, and it is why the web is civic.

Flower Law is a disk 42 meters from the plaza origin. Inside it, and off the Arena sand, a live blade becomes a flower. The Arena is the hole in the disk: center (0, 0, 18), eight meters of sand, where Arena Warden Gale keeps steel honest and poise is the lesson. Past 42 meters the Court ends. The warden wall stands at 56 meters. Pinewood Crossing, lettered only that way, sits on the salt road at (62, −28), a bazaar hold outside the wall. The animals sort themselves on those lines because the lines are real to them.

Inside the disk the pantry is dropped grain, archive mice, and flower-tide seed. Court pigeons work the roofs and the lantern iron. Lantern moths orbit the posts after the Lamplighter's round, each wing a single warm diamond of stored daylight. Market mousers work the crates and the steps. Nothing in that trio is trying to own the heart. A pigeon flock is a dotted arc. A moth is too small to be a conquest. A cat that only pounces mice is a janitor. The ground can tolerate janitors.

The Arena stays empty of them on purpose. It lies inside the 42 meter disk, so a bird on the sand would stand where steel still cuts. Gale's sentence is that the Court forbids conquest and the sand does not. An animal on the sand would turn that sentence into butchery with a legal blade. Pigeons may cross the air above it. They do not perch the rack. Moths do not orbit it. Cats, hares, stags, horses, and the wyrm do not path it.

Outside the disk the road is salt, pine, and ordinary steel. Hares take the shoulders at dawn and dusk. They freeze when they see flowers and bolt when they see steel, which makes the gate mouth a bad room for them and the verge a good one. Stags graze the pine line in a family of four or five, the lower end of their social range, rare in the roll and solid when they are actually there. Two wagon horses stand at the hitch. They are draft, braided in cloth when a delivery leads them through the plaza and back out before night. They are not the unnamed rider in Orin Rede's exhausted-mount book. That pattern remains the Impossible Print, three clues still unjoined, and this ecology does not spend it.

The salt wyrm is the one fight the road is allowed. One thick eel, a chunky brine collar, a spit glob that slows. It keeps the culvert under the milepost because the salt is its camouflage. It is a skirmisher with a tell, a wet inhale like a tide in a pipe. It is not a boss, not a larger cousin, and not Kiren's uncatalogued print. If it crosses 42 meters it turns. If the spit would land on the cobble, the glob crusts into white flowers and the slow never applies. The same law that took the conspirators' footing takes the wyrm's weapon.

The southern quarter of the lamps is thinner. Two years ago the Refusal Field over the hub collapsed and reformed in an afternoon, and the southern arc never matched the rest. Six months ago it weakened again. Captain Ren Solare noticed. Oracle Nesha confirmed. The Speaker has not told the Assembly. Moths, which only drink the light the posts are actually holding, make that wound visible without a speech. A northern post carries three to five diamonds. A southern post carries one or two. The count is a fact. The cause stays in Asbir's second notebook until someone whose office it is chooses to promote it.

Nobody censuses the pigeons. The Grid embassy reads what crosses its spine, and Kael studies gaps, and the Curators will not hand them a flock tally. To live here is already the ninth refusal: the refusal to let one's own refusal win. Numbering the birds that eat the goddess's seed would be a small conquest with a clipboard. Maren will file a moth count because a traveler saw it. She will not file a pigeon census because an embassy asked.

The Bazaar Consortium feeds the inner web by accident. Velka's row spills grain. Grain feeds pigeons and mice. Mice feed mousers. Velka would also price salt-hide and antler, and the Watch plus the verge rangers keep that price outside the wall. Kiren treats the verge the way a librarian treats a private collection. He mentions what matters. A stag is in the collection. An arrow at the milepost is not the use of it. The quest that teaches that already exists. This facet does not add a second one.

Founding Day is the one hour the pigeon cap opens from fourteen to twenty-four. The Law is read on the unpaved ring. Concordia stands at the warm apex, Concord faces her with a slate he does not write on, the Sovereign stands with his back to both. The birds rise because the plaza is full of people and seed and held breath. They settle when the reading ends. No sheath clears on that court. The flock is part of the quiet, not a parade float.

What the Court refuses to render, it refuses plainly. A long-legged civic bird in the old creature seed is not the pigeon, and it has no bible id, so it does not appear. The cistern amphibian has no id either. A missing mesh spawns nothing: no gull dressed as a pigeon, no wolf dressed as a stag, no Quaternius body dressed as anything. The hitch stays empty rather than lie. The ditch stays empty rather than borrow a basilisk. The milepost still says Pinewood Crossing.

## Court pigeon

They nest where the lantern brackets bolt into limestone, and the soot on the breast is lantern smoke, not a dye. The neck carries one restrained teal. At a hundred meters the flock is a dotted arc, which is the silhouette test passing. Children in the market know them as the birds that will not cross a live blade. The birds know the older fact: when a blade is drawn in the Court it is already a flower, and flowers are a startle, not a predator.

During the day one flock of up to fourteen works the roofs inside the law, with two more allowed on the gatehouse and none past the wall. At night four stay perched and the rest are simply gone, not culled. On Founding Day the arc fattens for the reading and thins after. They do not land on Gale's sand. Overhead is air. The rack is a weapon shelf.

Their story in politics is a refusal of the clipboard. Embassy staff have tried to treat the arc as a signal, a count of how many souls are in the plaza, a Grid habit worn as birdwatching. The Curators' answer is that the birds are not a ledger. Brackish feeds them crusts and does not name them. That is the correct relationship.

## Lantern moth

The Canon seed called this animal a lamplighter moth and gave it a blinding bloom when struck. The bible row is stricter and wins: one warm diamond, no damage, not a will-o'-the-wisp, not the Crucible's unclosed moth. Hub children still chase them. The chase is play. A strike in the Court is a flower and a broken orbit, and then the same post gathers them again.

They exist only where the Lamplighter's work is visible, inside the disk, dusk through the lamp hours. Twenty-four is the plaza clamp. Six of those belong to the southern quarter, which cannot fake a healthy lamp. The Lamplighter will not say the dome is failing. Lore gives each newcomer one true sentence and then withdraws him. If that sentence is still unspent, the sentence is the count, spoken once on the eastern path, and the three-lantern round does not buy a second sight of him. If the round is already under way or finished, he does not come back. Chalk ticks on the first eastern post carry the same three steps, left before dawn, and Maren still takes the two numbers. If the player attaches a cause, she sets the cause aside. Asbir, if shown the paper later, already knows which notebook a cause belongs in. The Speaker's silence is not the player's line to break in a moth quest. The other person the temple records once shared the lantern with is not named here.

They avoid the Arena because the rack is not a civic lamp. A diamond over the sand would read as a telegraph for a fight that is not theirs.

## Market mouser

Three cats, three territories: archive steps, and two stretches of the market wedge. They are solitary. A crimson thread on a collar is stall dye from the bazaar row, the color of cheap merchant cord, and Lady Seraphine's embassy does not own it. The scholars' joke, that the cats index mice the way the archive indexes letters, has been repeated enough that Asbir is tired of finding it written as if it were observed.

The watch he asks for is one hour and one pounce. The fact is the crate. The inference is the joke. The difference is the space between them. A player who strikes the cat gets a flower and an empty step, and the hour is void. A player who kills the mouse themselves has skipped the animal and failed the point. The cat's hostile flag in the animal file means it has a pounce verb. On this plaza the verb's target is a mouse. People are not the prey, and the flower disk is not the place to discover a new one.

Gale's sand does not get a cat. The market wedge stops before the Arena lip.

## Salt-road hare

Ears as one vertical mark, body a single pebble, salt in the whiskers. They belong to the shoulder between the gate and the Sundering approach, and to the pine road. Dawn and dusk are the full caps: four between the law and the wall, six further out. Midday halves that. Night beds most of them.

Inside the disk the ambient count is zero, because the animal's two reflexes are the Court's two materials. A flower freezes it. Steel bolts it. The gate mouth collects both, whenever someone walks a wreath home from Gale's lesson or rests a hand on a hilt out of habit. Kiren's quest is one stray, already frozen, walked out on empty hands. Pocket the wreath. Sheathe. Do not follow it all the way to Pinewood. The escort is a lesson in the boundary, not a hunt and not a pet.

He will not show the print he has been keeping from the Watch. That track is not a hare. Paying the player with a mystery they have not earned would collapse the Impossible Print into a tutorial.

## Pinewood stag

Tall wedge, deep chest, antlers like a tuning fork kept inside the body width so the hundred-meter read stays a deer and not a scribble. Salt rime on the fetlocks, court dust in the coat from the wind off the wall, lantern bounce on the flank at dusk. The group is four or five on the verge when the rare roll fires. Volume weight one means the roll is uncommon. It does not mean a single symbolic deer.

They stop at the wall. A herd on the urn stones would own Founding Day's sightline, and the Court has already refused to be owned. If a bolt carries one across the cobble, the antler shove becomes a flower-touch and the animal leaves. The shove's real use is on the road, making space when a hitch horse crowds a fawn or the wyrm takes too much of the ditch.

Hide and antler are table hooks for a life outside this quest line. Old Seam's milepost errand already tells the player to watch and not loose an arrow, and to let the post count even if the mesh is missing. Velka's price list does not overrule that. Kiren's collection does not overrule it either. The stag story in this facet is density and refusal, not a kill.

The bible says Kael returned along this verge. The Court does not print which Kael on the spawn card. Zero studies the Sovereign's gap from inside the embassy ring. The verge is a walk, not his hunt, and the stag is not his evidence.

## Wagon horse

Two at the hitch past the law, Pinewood side or the gate shoulder, tack optional, harness gall visible, no horn and no armor grown into the hide. A flower braid, when one is led in with a delivery, is cloth. Riding is a different binder and a different honesty: the mount file already says the controller is absent. This slice does not sneak a seated camera in through an ecology cap.

Orin Rede knows every ordinary horse by temperament and water. His other book, the mounts brought back exhausted past explanation, stays shut. Those pages are the second clue of a chain this facet does not investigate.

## Salt wyrm

Primary body an eel, thick enough to beach, one donor trait in the crystal collar, one hook in the spit. That is the hybrid law, and it is why there is no second head and no gaze that turns people to salt. The glob is visible. The inhale is the telegraph. Break the collar and the spit stops, which is the weak point, and also the outcome Kiren does not want from a traveler with a lantern.

One animal. Dusk and night in the open ditch, day in the culvert, still the same creature. It does not enter the plaza. It does not enter the Arena, where the law would suddenly permit steel and a ditch-eel would be promoted into a boss by geography alone. Fantasy may field the same id on its own road. This slice does not move that weight.

The quest that shifts it comes only after the milepost has been read, so the name Pinewood Crossing is already in the player's mouth. Light in the ditch, space given, collar left whole. Chase it back into the flower disk and the quest fails, because the spit dying on the cobble means the warden line was used as a weapon. Maim it and Kiren will still take the report, without the Watch's thanks. An empty ditch, mesh missing, is a clear post. He logs that and does not ask for a stand-in.

## Side quests, short

**Count the Diamonds.** The Lamplighter's backstory is the hub's own: a lineage that may once have been two people and one lantern, and this quest still does not name the other. A single true sentence for the newly arrived, and no second meeting. If the sentence is unspent, it is the count. If Three Lanterns already took the meeting, the count is chalk on the first eastern post and he stays off the path. The southern thinness is the dome wound wearing an animal. The player is a witness, which is the office the Court actually has for guests.

**The Third Crate.** Asbir has kept the archive's side of the Lattice Correspondence for years and lost a wife when the dome failed in Year 89. He is not sentimental about cats. He is severe about jokes that impersonate observations. The mouser gives him a clean hour: a body, a crate, a mouse, three books.

**Empty Hands.** Kiren was raised by the verge rangers after he was orphaned, and he has tracked everything within fifty leagues that the catalogue admits. The hare is admitted. Walking it out is neighborhood trust, the Watch's value, practiced by a man who is not of the Watch. His unreported print stays unreported.

**Move the Wyrm.** The road's curse-rumor is a culvert with a tide-sound in it. Old Seam would call that a poor use of the word curse. Kiren calls it a salt wyrm and asks for a lamp. The backstory of the animal is the salt road itself: brine, pine needles stuck to the flank, a collar that is the whole tell. Moving it keeps Pinewood's name attached to a place travelers can stand.
