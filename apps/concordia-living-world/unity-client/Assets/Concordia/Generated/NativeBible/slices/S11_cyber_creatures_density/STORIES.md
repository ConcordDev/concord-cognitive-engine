# Stories — The Grid counts, and some bodies are the remainder

## The law on the ground

The Grid's refusal is numbers. The gate calls it the Refusal of Numbers. The world line is shorter: I will not be counted. The no under that line is that identity is terrain. A name is a place a machine can stand. Give the Census a finished sum and you have handed it a floor.

Steel is live. People fight Capoeira. The pulse is the cut. A rifle in ambient hands is outside the density law, and outside this slice. Flower Law is the Hub disk of 42 m. A blade dragged back inside that disk, and outside the Arena, is a flower. The deck is not that disk. Pinewood Crossing is a Hub verge at (62, −28). Nothing in this facet stands there.

Damage from a census drone hides until you let the number exist. That hide is how the hit is shown. The hit still resolves. Winning the bar by completing a sum still fails the refusal. The Census fight already says so. These smaller windows say the same thing at the size of a deck, a clinic door, and a dog.

## What the city is eating

Kael Nakamura went into a consultant immersion and came out distributed across the substrate. Public services measure better when his processes run. Civil liberties measure worse. Nyx Torres was a corporate enforcer, found the Augmented Children file, and left. Seventeen children had been returned to their families with no disclosure. Her first city-block blackout lasted two minutes and seventeen seconds. Five hundred and twelve people lost power. Three medical devices failed. Two patients lived because the medics had been warned. Kael felt that block as an amputation. He has not forgotten it. Later she had a clean shot at his secondary cradle and did not take it. Three days later his clones reached her safe-house threshold and he recalled them. Neither has explained it in public.

That pair is the politics of the city. This facet is the ecology that grew in the argument. The Census wants a total. The machines that serve the total are a disc, a door, and a frame that was supposed to have been deleted. The lives that are not in the total are a dog and a bird. The argument is not a mood. It is where bodies are allowed to stand.

The Week the Quarter Went Dark is seven days, three deaths, two riots, and one birth in the noodle shop on 7th. The cause was never declared. This slice does not declare it, and it does not put a mob on a quarter that has no coordinate. Ghost-7 is a voice on a stolen frequency that saved a child and vanished. Silver Vey wrote his first forged identity at sixteen, for his sister, and she left under that name. Those are people and papers. They are not spawn rows.

Hidden truths stay hidden. The Upload's private authorship, the funders behind the children program, and what was actually traded in the Voss visit are not quest text and not board lines.

## Quest backstories

**The sum stays dark.** A cable tech on the Zero deck keeps their own name off the Census and still has to watch the discs try to finish other people's sums. They have seen a finished sum become a floor the next night: the same acrylic, a new ping, a person who walks it because the number was allowed to exist. They want the lead lens broken while the other two digits are still just paint. They do not want a sparrow in the swing, and they do not want the practice-frame lesson taught again. The pulse was already earned elsewhere, or it was not. The quest is the witness of an unfinished sum.

**The serial stays ground.** The clinic keeps no file Nevex can subpoena. The construct at the door is a patient in the only sense the docs trust: something was done to it, the record was removed, and closing the record would make the injury legible to the count. The tech who coils cable at that door has watched the frame try, every few mornings, to engrave itself back into the system that ground the serial off. They want a witness who will not help it succeed. A stamped blank fails the errand. A dead frame fails it too. The walk away, still blank, is the turn-in.

**The tick skips.** The officer in the gap wears the bar down and the coat on, and still refuses to finish a count. Density already stations that role on counts they will not close. This one was given the gap because the open meters are the only ground where a miss is not immediately a defect in somebody's circle. They know the plated dog is not the one-eared dog. They want the skip seen, and they want the stray's scar left as skin. Fitting a port, or killing either dog to simplify the report, is the failure. They will not write the skip down as a total. The slate stays blank. Another quest already hands out slates. This one does not.

The three givers are crowd roles inside the labor budget of 6 and the officer budget of 2. Nyx, Kira, Silver, Lavren, Holt, Zero, Iris, Old Dom, and the rest of the named list are not copied onto these points to make a quest start.
