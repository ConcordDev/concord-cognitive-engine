# Stories — the Court that refuses a government

## Politics

The Unburned Court is the battlefield left unpaved after the Truce. Concord built the Link to watch what Concordia made. The Sovereign refused to let the eight surviving gates close, because he had already refused the idea of alone. The Concordant Law set the terms that followed: each world keeps one embassy at its gate, staffed by its own, answerable to no pillar. The gates stand on a ring 34 meters out. Flower Law holds to 42. The wall is at 56. Anyone powerful at home walks here as a guest. The three pillars are the permanent authority, and they are not a crown.

Once, in Year 38, three embassies tried to own the heart. They held the plaza for four hours. Concordia rose through the flagstones as root and bloom and carried them out. No one died. No one was punished. The idiom that survived is a tide of flowers: a plan that fails gently and absolutely. That is why the politics of Year 91 are leverage, secrets, debts, and trade. A health-bar boss in the disk would be a second attempt at the same conquest, and the ground has already answered.

The playable city is these radii. Lore calls the hub a walled world the size of a country. The instance the guests actually stand in is the Canon court. When Elias says he works two streets over from the Sovereign's tower, the two streets this slice can walk are the two legs between his stand at (−9.5, 8.2) and the Sovereign's back at (8.6, 0.3). A country of basements is not spawned to make the line literal.

## The five seats

The Compact that has rows in the faction file is five seats, not eight embassies.

The Assembly sits beside the Resonance Stone. Mira Lattice convenes it. Term-limited, slow by design, no single crown. She knows, because Nesha told her, that the Refusal Field's southern arc may fail again inside the year. She has not put that to a vote. Ren Solare knows the same weakening and has not trusted the Watch with it. Asbir lost a wife when the field collapsed and reformed in Year 89. Those three facts are the live wound. None of the ten quests publishes them. A politics that "solves" the Speaker's silence in an afternoon has skipped her office.

The Curators keep the archive. Asbir's three notebooks are the method: fact, inference, difference. Maren writes what she sees and will not promote an embassy's claim into a sighting. The sealed Voss genealogy is in the deepest vault and opens with the Three's leave, which these quests do not ask. The cavity behind his audience wall is a different secret, already a quest, about a letter from Tunya. Guest politics in the plaza do not pick that lock.

The Watch is Ren's. Gale is the Iron Wardens' face on the sand, open helm, estoc only there, flower when he steps into the cobble. The faction row is `concordant_watch`. The lore tag `iron_wardens` is how the ring-of-doors story and the fight picker say the same people. Merging Gale and Ren into one captain would hand him her secret and her streets. The shoulder patrol between the law and the wall is six wardens, CX, accountable to the Assembly. They hail. They are not a camp to clear.

The Bazaar Consortium is Velka's seat. Haggling is the craft. The Market Well has no polygon in the city file, so this slice does not draw one. Vesper, at (7.2, 9.1), funds half the charities and owns the debt of the other half. She is a guest of the Dawn, not the master of the row. Brackish's loaf is already a quest. A second bread errand would only restate it.

The Refusal Keep is Nesha's. Lyra, at (5.5, 14.8), keeps the second hour and will not teach a ninth. The keepers of the upper grove think she is withholding a lesson. The ninth refusal is the hub itself: to enter, to put a coin down in a market that belongs to no world, is to refuse to let one's own refusal win. It is stood upon. Old Seam still follows Nesha at a distance of twenty years. That distance already has a quest that refuses to close it.

## Guests, and the names that are not seats

Eight figures move through the present tense of the lore, and the Canon list is longer than eight because the Court also keeps its own people.

Elias Voss runs the Anti-Sovereign from the work of standing where the tower can be seen and not entered. He and Seraphine share a name, a bloodline neither will discuss, and a mutual surveillance. The Voss Question — four worlds, one sealed ancestor, a first name nobody who has read it will say — is the setting's stake about whether agency stays in many hands. This slice keeps the stake sealed. Elias's walk succeeds when the Sovereign does not turn. Being noticed by him is the rarest status the hub knows, and a side quest must not mint it.

Seraphine's weapon is etiquette. Deep red in a glove and a lining, a wooden fan that stays closed, a smile that is a face and not a mask. The Crimson Court embassy trades in blackmail dressed as courtesy. The courtesy this quest allows is an objection said aloud at the Sundering plaque, under Flower Law, followed by a refusal to sell a name the player has not read. If the player invents the ancestor's name, the fan quest fails and Asbir will not file the invention as a fact.

Jax takes contracts from all eight and loyalty from none. Eight cords, frayed, one per gate color. The Frontier cord is the important one: that gate at (−34, 0) has a plaque and no staff, because the walkers say the road is their door. The other seven find the absence insulting or enviable. Walking the plaques in Canon order and signing nothing is the whole quest. The shadow network is a lore tag on the founding of the ring. It is not a faction row, and this slice does not elect Jax its minister.

Mama Iron Rose holds her people across realities by never sleeping in the same world twice. In the Court the knife is a flower already in her hand, and the rose pin is the emblem that can be given. The Crime gate sits at 34 meters, eight meters inside the law, so a dramatic draw at the plaque would still flower. She walks the player past the plaque to the lip at (−29.7, −29.7) and stops. Reputation is what the Syndicate launders here. Money is a different world's bill. Past the lip is steel, the Watch, and a road this quest does not take.

Nyx organizes the uncounted from the same embassy as Zero, shorter, tools on the shoulder, a slate with the names scratched out.

Thorne comes for the grove, curse folded inward, sword only outside the plaza. He is not Seraphine's champion. His walk stops at the Tunya plaque, inside the law, and does not stock Upper Grove. The keepers there still think Lyra is hiding a lesson. She walks the unpaved court with the player and speaks no ninth line. The hour can be recognized. It is not granted by the walk.

Vesper, at (7.2, 9.1), funds half the charities and owns the debt of the other half. The basket and the ledger stay in different hands. One licensed stall stands in for the Market Well, which has no polygon. She does not call the debt. Brackish's loaf remains its own errand. The Dawn plaque is where she shows what a finishing blow would be, and then does not take it.

Zero studies the gap where a count should be and is not. His errand asks for a number and accepts only the refusal on the Grid stone. Nyx's scratched slate, if it is in play, stays hers. Giving it to him fails both quests, because his study would turn her scratched names back into entries. They are not a romance and not a boss pair.

The Lamplighter keeps the eastern path and his one true sentence. Brackish already decided about bread. Gale already taught the urn. Asbir already separated the notebooks. Those lives continue. They do not get a second tutorial.

Concordia stands at the warm apex with open hands and flowers that wilt like real flowers. Concord faces her and measures. The Sovereign keeps his back to both. The Founding Day reading is the year-scale version, and it already forbids anyone to name the arrangement. The ordinary-day version is three lengths: 12.6 meters between Concord and Concordia, 10.43 between Concord and the Sovereign, 10.90 between Concordia and the Sovereign. Maren files the numbers. Concord's slate stays blank. That is enough, which is his line, and it is the turn-in.

## Uncounted Wharf

The wharf is a blend, not a district of the Court. Iron Coast brick meets Grid acrylic on one wet asphalt floor. Fiber runs inside Coast pipes. Neon banners are cloth, soot-stained, and the sodium lamps still win the color of the rain. Census drones are legally not supposed to count there, and they do. `hyb_billdrone` is allowed on that asphalt. Flower Law is not.

Nyx can say that sentence because her work is the people those drones tally. Mama's hem can still be wet from a dock she slept beside on another night. Zero can want the gap closed. None of that puts the wharf inside the 56 meter wall or gives it a Hub coordinate. The kit has none. Dragging Grid neon into the plaza is forbidden by the intersection rules, and lettering a Court ring "Uncounted Wharf" would steal Pinewood's honesty for a different sin: a place renamed until it is nowhere. The quest stops at the Grid plaque. The membrane is not a door this slice opens.

## The count that cannot stand

When Nyx's quest is accepted, one Census officer steps off the Grid gate. They are already inside the 42 meter disk, because the gate is. The pulseblade becomes a flower on the first step toward the origin. The ground carries them back onto the membrane. No health table, no loot, no kill. It is the Year 38 tide at the size of a single visitor who tried to write a Court name onto a slate. Striking them, chasing them onto Gale's sand, or following them through the membrane fails the quest. The pigeons are not part of the tally. S01 already refused a bird census, and this facet does not reopen it.

## The press, and the cord on the shoulder

When Seraphine asks for an objection said aloud, five people come to hear it: three licensed merchants and two court laborers, CX, inside the disk, off the sand. They are the courtesy press. Etiquette without witnesses is only a private smile. They have no health and nothing to loot. The fan staying shut sends them back to work. A drawn weapon fails her quest and clears them without a corpse.

On the Frontier spoke, at (−46, 0), one shadow courier waits with a ninth cord. Forty-six meters is past the lip and inside the wall, so steel is live and the lantern throw is a real throw. They hail. They want a signature Jax's circuit exists to refuse. The preferred ending is the refusal: they leave. Attacking them on the shoulder is a scuffle, capoeira, satchel square at the hip, no mask, and it is a loss of the politics even if the courier falls. There is no loot and no remembrance. Jax's own quest still fails if the player steps out to meet them. If they are dragged back inside 42 meters, off Gale's sand, the throw becomes a flower and they walk themselves back to the shoulder. That is not the Year 38 tide. It is one person the ground will not let finish a contract in the Court.

## The shoulder and the stalls

Six wardens walk the shoulder where steel is legal, between the lip and the wall. If one of them steps back into the disk, off the sand, the sword in their hand is a flower until they step out. Their captain is Ren, who is not in the guest list. Their sand is Gale's, and they do not crowd it. Four stall merchants work inside the law with a flower in the buttonhole and a brass scale. They sell under Velka's license. A steel sale does not complete on that cobble. They are the civic texture of the Well, not a mob to farm.

## What stays unwritten

The Speaker's vote, the genealogy's first name, the cavity, the twenty-year gap, the Impossible Print, and the Frontier's missing staff all remain what they were. Pinewood Crossing keeps its name. The salt wyrm, the stag, and the moth count stay in the ecology slice. A missing CX mesh spawns nothing: no Quaternius envoy, no borrowed sentinel dressed as a Census officer, no billdrone walked into the plaza to give the wharf a body it does not have here.
